New Baltimore is a waterfront city in Macomb County, in Metro Detroit's orbit, sitting on Anchor Bay — the northern part of Lake St. Clair.

It is not a random rural Michigan town. It is not Detroit. It is not “up north.”

It is a small, residential, lakefront community with downtown streets, subdivisions, cars, local businesses, a community college, parks, marinas, sports, and a lot of people who know the same people.

WHEN SOMEONE SAYS A ROAD NAME

M-29

THE LAKESHORE ROAD

M-29 runs along the lakeshore through the New Baltimore area. It is the road for driving around without necessarily having somewhere to go.

You get roadside businesses, residential driveways, storefronts, streetlights, and glimpses of the lake. At night, it becomes headlights, storefront glow, pavement, and water between the trees.

WASHINGTON

THE DOWNTOWN SPINE

Washington Street is the main commercial street through downtown.

Older buildings sit close to the sidewalk. Local businesses, residences, errands, students, parents, and people you recognize overlap here.

FRONT

THE WATERFRONT EDGE

Front Street runs along the waterfront side of downtown and connects the town-center area toward Burke Park and the water.

More open than Washington Street. More lake. More boats. More people wandering around in summer.

23 / 24

THE OUTER SUBURBAN RING

23 Mile and 24 Mile are where the environment gets more suburban and commercial: wider roads, parking lots, strip malls, fast food, corporate signs, planned housing, and car-dependent infrastructure.

This is not the intimate downtown waterfront. It is the broader suburban part of the area.

I-94

THE WAY OUT

I-94 connects New Baltimore with the wider suburban and Detroit-area road network, including routes toward Detroit and Port Huron.

It is interstate infrastructure: multiple lanes, barriers, overhead signs, billboards, headlights, traffic, and highway noise.

THE WATER IS NOT BACKDROP DECORATION

ANCHOR BAY

Anchor Bay is part of Lake St. Clair. It is the waterfront defining the town's summer life.

Boats. Docks. Swimmers. Fishing. Wind coming off the water. Wet pavement after summer storms. Lake water, sunscreen, cut grass, and food cooking outside.

The weather matters because the water is actually part of where people go.

SCHOOL

ABCC

ANCHOR BAY COMMUNITY COLLEGE 2015

ABCC is the college setting for this series.

Classes, parking lots, lunch, people leaving early, and the constant question of what everybody is doing tonight.

School is not an isolated bubble. People move between campus, downtown, the lake, cars, restaurants, friends' houses, and other local places.

LATE JUNE

BAY-RAMA

Five days of the waterfront and downtown basically getting taken over by the festival.

Carnival midway. Festival tents. Live music. Food stalls. Game booths. Community groups. Kids. Families. Teenagers. Volunteers. Local businesses.

There is a parade through the streets and fireworks over Anchor Bay near Burke Park.

It is intensely local. People from the community are not treating it like some distant tourist attraction. It is their summer event.

IMPORTANT LOCAL HORROR

FISHFLIES

Fishfly season can be sparse until suddenly there are fishflies everywhere.

They end up on the asphalt and under car tires. The tires make the characteristic pop pop pop pop sound.

During the season, friends may literally lower the windows, talk shit, and deliberately drive through dense patches as a stupid local summer ritual.
