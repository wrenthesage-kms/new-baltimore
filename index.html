THE SHORT VERSION

where the hell are we?

new baltimore sits on anchor bay in macomb county, close enough to metro detroit that nobody here thinks of it as some isolated little lake town.

it's waterfront suburbs, old downtown buildings, residential streets, marinas, parks, school parking lots, strip malls, m-29, i-94, and everybody running into somebody they know.

ABCC is right in the middle of that social web. classes are small. people recognize each other. professors know names. students see each other downtown, at the lake, at restaurants, at parties, in parking lots, and sitting in somebody else's car for absolutely no reason.

nothing needs to become a huge event. most things are just something to do.

★ important

ANCHOR BAY COMMUNITY COLLEGE

ABCC

THE TOWN / MACOMB COUNTY

new baltimore

lakefront suburb. old downtown. metro detroit orbit.

new baltimore sits along anchor bay at the northern end of lake st. clair, close enough to metro detroit that the town never feels isolated from everything around it.

the waterfront, downtown, residential neighborhoods, highways, commercial strips, and lake roads all overlap. you can spend twenty minutes at the water, get in a car, and suddenly be looking at strip malls and chain stores.

that's part of the point. new baltimore isn't one aesthetic. it's a small lakefront community sitting directly inside the suburban sprawl around it.

04.01
COMMERCIAL CORE

downtown

washington st // front st // green st // maria

downtown is the old core of new baltimore. buildings sit close to the sidewalk. businesses, homes, restaurants, and storefronts overlap instead of being separated into one giant planned district.

washington street is the main spine. front street runs along the waterfront. smaller streets branch away from that core.

it is lived-in, a little uneven, and very easy to accidentally run into somebody.

ROLEPLAY USE the collision zone

classmates. parents. professors. neighbors. people from the restaurant. somebody's cousin. somebody who knows somebody.

LOCAL RULE roman is almost never anonymous here.

somebody is probably going to recognize him.

04.02
MAIN STREET

washington street

storefronts // sidewalks // downtown traffic

washington is the street where downtown actually feels connected. older buildings and local businesses create a tighter street environment than the commercial corridors outside town.

during the day, there are people walking for errands, students moving around, parents, business owners, and residents who have somewhere to be.

during bay-rama, the normal street disappears under people, tents, food, music, carnival traffic, and everybody else's business.

04.03
WATERFRONT EDGE

front street

downtown → waterfront → burke park

front street is where downtown gives way to the bay. the street sits closer to the water and the atmosphere opens up.

boats, docks, park activity, lake wind, people walking, and summer traffic make this feel different from the tighter commercial streets.

it works as a threshold. town behind you. water ahead.

GOOD FOR getting out of downtown without actually leaving.

quieter conversations. walking. waiting. sitting around. looking at the water.

WATERFRONT HUB

walter & mary burke park

beach // dock // boardwalk // fishing // swimming

burke park is the town's living room.

it has direct access to anchor bay, a beach and swimming area, fishing, picnic spaces, playground areas, a boardwalk, and the long waterfront dock.

the important part for roleplay is that it is public. there are people around. families. teenagers. fishermen. couples. walkers. kids. people sitting around doing nothing.

you can have privacy here without being isolated. the farther you move toward the water, the more the town opens up around you.

04.05
CONSTANT ENVIRONMENT

anchor bay

lake st. clair // boats // wind // weather

the bay is part of the background of life here. it does not need to become the plot every time someone walks outside.

boats move through the water. docks stay busy. people fish. people swim. lake wind changes how hot the afternoon feels.

summer brings lake water, sunscreen, cut grass, food, humidity, and the occasional cool breeze. after a storm, pavement stays wet and the whole waterfront smells different.

BACKGROUND NOISE

04.06
RESIDENTIAL

the neighborhoods

houses // driveways // cul-de-sacs // back roads

residential new baltimore is where the social pressure of downtown drops off.

houses, driveways, lawns, side streets, subdivisions, older homes, and quieter stretches sit behind the waterfront core.

some roads end in wooded little pockets where there isn't much reason for anyone to be except the people who live there.

which makes them useful when a car full of teenagers wants to sit somewhere without an audience.

04.07
DEVELOPING SPORTS COMPLEX

festival park

fields // baseball // gravel // construction

in 2015, festival park is still developing into the more structured recreational complex it will eventually become.

it feels more open and spread out than downtown. grass, dirt, gravel paths, chain-link fencing, baseball diamonds, portable bleachers, equipment, and the general visual language of a place still being built up.

you hear bats, whistles, people talking, cut grass, dirt, and hot asphalt. outside the activity, the surrounding residential quiet becomes noticeable.

WHY PEOPLE END UP HERE more space. less downtown.

sports. hanging around. messing with each other. conversations that don't need an audience.

04.08
OUTER SUBURBAN RING

23 / 24 mile

stores // parking lots // traffic // chains

23 and 24 mile are where new baltimore starts feeling less like the waterfront town and more like the larger suburban infrastructure around it.

roads get wider. parking lots get bigger. storefronts become more standardized. chain stores, coffee shops, fast food, errands, gas stations, and new construction become normal.

this is where you go because you need something. it is useful. it is convenient. it doesn't necessarily feel like anywhere.

05.01
LAKESHORE ROUTE

m-29

drive // lake glimpses // storefronts // no destination

m-29 runs along the lakefront and connects new baltimore to the surrounding communities. it is a real road people use, not a scenic attraction.

stretches of water, residential driveways, trees, roadside businesses, storefront glow, cracked sidewalks, and ordinary traffic all pass by.

at night, streetlights and storefronts cut through the darker stretches. the lake is sometimes visible. sometimes it's just road.

NO DESTINATION MODE
keep driving. nobody has to say anything.
downtown → m-29 → somewhere → keep going
05.02
INTERSTATE

i-94

detroit ↔ port huron ↔ suburbs

i-94 is functional. it is not the pretty road.

multiple lanes, barriers, medians, signs, billboards, headlights, highway noise, tire white noise, and the stale air inside a car that has been running for an hour.

it gets you somewhere. it also gives people an excuse to keep driving.

fishfly season

downtown → washington → green → front → burke park

the town gets taken over.

bay-rama isn't some detached tourist event that happens to be near town.

it is a community event. families are there. teenagers are there. local businesses are involved. volunteers are involved. classmates are involved. everybody knows somebody working a booth.

downtown gets crowded enough that disappearing into the crowd becomes possible. unfortunately, it also means you are constantly running into people you know.
